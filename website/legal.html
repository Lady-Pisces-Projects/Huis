Last Updated: November 17, 2026

Security Built for Legal Work

Legal matters can contain some of the most sensitive information an organization handles.

HuisLaw is designed for attorneys, law firms, legal staff, and clients working with information that may include:

  • attorney-client communications;

  • attorney work product;

  • evidence;

  • discovery;

  • confidential records;

  • financial information;

  • court information;

  • personal information;

  • privileged legal strategy.

Security and confidentiality are therefore core product requirements for HuisLaw—not optional features.

Our approach is based on defense in depth, meaning sensitive information should be protected across identity, applications, infrastructure, storage, integrations, AI processing, evidence workflows, and user permissions.

1. Customer Ownership and Control

The law firm owns its data. HuisLaw processes it on the firm’s behalf.

Customers control who is authorized to access their organization and legal Matters.

Depending on product configuration, Customers may control:

  • users;

  • roles;

  • Matter permissions;

  • client portal access;

  • privileged folders;

  • connected email accounts;

  • email folders and labels;

  • cloud-storage connections;

  • third-party integrations;

  • exports;

  • AI functionality;

  • automation rules.

2. Privilege and Confidentiality

HuisLaw is designed to help law firms organize and restrict access to sensitive legal information.

Materials may be designated inside HuisLaw as:

  • Attorney-Client Privileged;

  • Attorney Work Product;

  • Confidential;

  • Highly Confidential;

  • Internal Firm Only;

  • Protective Order;

  • Client Accessible;

  • Expert Accessible;

  • Restricted.

These classifications help control access and organization.

A designation inside HuisLaw does not itself create attorney-client privilege or another legal protection.

The attorney or law firm remains responsible for legal determinations regarding privilege and confidentiality.

3. Identity and Access Security

HuisLaw’s security architecture is designed around individual accountability and controlled access.

Controls may include:

  • unique user accounts;

  • role-based access controls;

  • Matter-level permissions;

  • administrative access management;

  • strong authentication;

  • multi-factor authentication where supported;

  • secure session management;

  • login monitoring;

  • timely removal of terminated or unauthorized users;

  • audit logging.

Enterprise plans may support additional identity controls as they become available.

4. Encryption

HuisLaw’s production security architecture is designed to use industry-standard encryption protections.

These may include:

Data in transit: HTTPS/TLS.

Data at rest: encryption for databases, object storage, backups, and other protected storage.

Secrets: secure handling of API credentials, encryption keys, OAuth tokens, and application secrets.

High-sensitivity information: additional field-level or object-level protections where appropriate.

5. Tenant Isolation

HuisLaw is designed as a multi-tenant legal platform.

Controls should prevent one law firm or Customer from accessing another Customer’s data.

Authorization should be enforced at both the application and data layers using the Customer’s:

  • organization;

  • location;

  • user identity;

  • role;

  • Matter permissions;

  • resource permissions.

6. Matter-Level Security

Legal information should not automatically become available to every user in a firm.

HuisLaw is designed to support Matter-level restrictions so firms can limit access to specific:

  • Matters;

  • folders;

  • documents;

  • evidence;

  • communications;

  • AI sessions;

  • financial information;

  • privileged material.

7. Evidence Integrity and Chain of Custody

HuisLaw’s evidence architecture is designed to preserve an original source file while allowing attorneys to work with separate derivative copies.

Working copies may be:

  • annotated;

  • highlighted;

  • redacted;

  • organized;

  • converted;

  • marked as exhibits.

The underlying original should remain preserved.

Evidence-integrity functions may include:

  • cryptographic file hashing;

  • upload timestamps;

  • uploader identity;

  • original filenames;

  • file metadata;

  • version history;

  • chain-of-custody events;

  • access history;

  • duplicate detection;

  • near-duplicate detection;

  • export history;

  • exhibit designations.

These tools support legal workflows but do not independently determine admissibility or authenticity.

8. Artificial Intelligence and Customer Data

HuisLaw does not use Customer Content to train generalized AI models for other Customers or the public.

HuisLaw may use third-party AI providers as subprocessors to provide requested AI functionality.

Providers and configurations are selected with the goal of:

  • maintaining confidentiality;

  • limiting processing to authorized purposes;

  • preventing Customer Content from entering generalized model-training datasets;

  • protecting Customer data through appropriate contractual and technical controls.

9. Human Oversight of AI

Artificial intelligence is not a replacement for attorney judgment.

HuisLaw is designed to keep qualified legal professionals in control.

Material AI output should distinguish, where appropriate:

FACT — directly supported by source material.

INFERENCE — an interpretation derived from available facts.

AI SUGGESTION — a generated recommendation or strategy.

LEGAL AUTHORITY — an identified legal source.

UNKNOWN — information that cannot be established from available evidence.

Attorneys should review and verify AI-generated legal work before relying on it.

10. Source Traceability

Where supported by the applicable feature, HuisLaw is designed to connect material AI conclusions back to their underlying sources.

For example, an attorney should be able to move from:

“Employer received notice on April 18.”

to the email, document, testimony, or other evidence supporting that statement.

Source traceability is an important safeguard against unsupported AI conclusions.

11. Case Outcome Intelligence

HuisLaw may provide advanced Case Outcome Intelligence that analyzes:

  • case strengths;

  • weaknesses;

  • legal elements;

  • evidence;

  • contradictory evidence;

  • missing information;

  • discovery gaps;

  • damages;

  • applicable legal authority;

  • potential case-value scenarios.

These features are decision-support tools.

They do not guarantee:

  • success;

  • settlement;

  • verdict;

  • judicial ruling;

  • damages award;

  • particular case value.

Qualified attorneys remain responsible for legal judgment.

12. Logging, Monitoring, and Auditability

HuisLaw’s architecture is designed to maintain records appropriate to sensitive legal workflows.

These may include:

  • authentication logs;

  • access logs;

  • administrative actions;

  • integration activity;

  • API activity;

  • evidence provenance;

  • security events;

  • exports;

  • permission changes;

  • system activity.

13. Secure Software Development

HuisLaw’s development process is designed around secure software-development practices.

These may include:

  • source control;

  • branch review;

  • code review;

  • automated testing;

  • dependency management;

  • vulnerability review;

  • environment separation;

  • least-privilege access;

  • security testing;

  • secure secrets management.

Real client evidence, privileged documents, production credentials, and confidential legal information should never be stored in public source-code repositories.

14. Malware and File Security

Uploaded information may be subject to malware scanning, file validation, processing safeguards, and security checks before being made available within the platform.

Large or unusual files may require additional processing.

15. Backup, Recovery, and Resilience

HuisLaw’s infrastructure is designed to include appropriate backup and recovery safeguards.

Depending on the service and deployment, controls may include:

  • encrypted backups;

  • redundancy;

  • recovery procedures;

  • availability monitoring;

  • disaster-recovery processes.

Specific recovery objectives or availability commitments may be stated in enterprise agreements where applicable.

16. Integrations and Subprocessors

HuisLaw may use third-party providers for services such as:

  • cloud infrastructure;

  • storage;

  • AI processing;

  • authentication;

  • payments;

  • communications;

  • monitoring;

  • analytics;

  • court information;

  • integrations.

Providers are evaluated based on the sensitivity of the information they process and the function they perform.

When a Customer independently connects a third-party integration, that third party’s terms and security practices may also apply.

17. Security Incidents

If HuisLaw identifies a security incident affecting Customer information, we will take reasonable steps to:

  • investigate;

  • contain the incident;

  • remediate vulnerabilities;

  • preserve appropriate records;

  • notify affected Customers when required by law or contract.

Suspected unauthorized account access should be reported immediately to:

info@huislaw.ai

18. Responsible Vulnerability Disclosure

If you believe you have discovered a security vulnerability in HuisLaw, please email:

info@huislaw.ai

Please include:

  • a description of the issue;

  • affected feature or URL;

  • steps to reproduce the issue;

  • supporting information that can safely be provided.

Do not intentionally access, download, alter, destroy, or disclose information belonging to another Customer while conducting security research.

19. Compliance and Independent Assurance

HuisLaw’s security and compliance program will evolve as the platform grows.

HuisLaw will only claim a specific certification, independent audit, compliance assessment, or security designation when that certification or assessment has actually been completed and is current.

Enterprise Customers may contact us regarding available:

  • security documentation;

  • vendor questionnaires;

  • subprocessors;

  • data-processing terms;

  • security reviews;

  • procurement requirements.

20. Customer Security Responsibilities

Security is shared between HuisLaw and its Customers.

Customers should:

  • use strong authentication;

  • maintain unique accounts;

  • avoid password sharing;

  • remove former users promptly;

  • carefully configure Matter permissions;

  • verify recipients before exporting or transmitting legal information;

  • review integrations and connected services;

  • maintain secure endpoint devices;

  • train personnel regarding phishing and credential security;

  • independently verify AI-generated legal work.

21. No Absolute Security Guarantee

No technology provider can guarantee that a system will never experience a security incident.

HuisLaw maintains and develops safeguards intended to reduce risk, protect Customer Content, and respond appropriately when security concerns arise.
